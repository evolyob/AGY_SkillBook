# Dialogue & Scene Craft (`deep-story`)

> Scope: Loaded on demand when drafting high-tension dialogue, legal/investigative interrogations, power-asymmetric confrontations, or subtext-driven scenes.

## 1. Core Dialogue Rules

Every dialogue exchange MUST carry at least one function (delete or summarize in narration otherwise):
1. **Advance Plot**: Reveal new evidence, trigger a commitment, or shift stakes.
2. **Raise Expectation**: Plant a ticking question or tension.
3. **Establish Voice**: Distinguish speakers without relying on name tags.

At emotional peaks, each line MUST catch the previous line's emotional pressure (push back, deflect, yield, or freeze). Pure sequential Q&A reads like a transcript.

### Forbidden Dialogue Anti-Patterns
- Supporting characters praising the protagonist without factual/stake basis.
- Characters explaining facts both speakers (and the reader) already know.
- Uniform voices across different ages, ranks, or professions.
- High-status characters giving long defensive speeches instead of executing leverage.
- Dialogue prematurely explaining away suspense before the scene turns.

---

## 2. Power-Asymmetric Dialogue Patterns

Line length signals leverage: the party holding power delivers short facts or outcomes; the losing party over-explains or blusters.

| Mode | Structural Pattern | Chinese Anchor Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Suppression** | Opponent blusters with seniority/rules -> Protagonist closes with an already-executed fact | `少东家合上账本：“库房卯时就换了锁，周账房刚把你那本私账交出来。”` |
| **Reversal** | Opponent flaunts borrowed leverage -> Protagonist reveals they control the source | `“你妈给你那张副卡挂在我主卡名下，十分钟前停了。”` |
| **Shut-Down** | Truth already exposed; protagonist shrinks replies until cutting off (`嗯` -> `隨意`) | `她把签好的离婚协议放上鞋柜：“随意。”` |

---

## 3. Subtext & Hidden Agendas

Subtext is the gap between **what the speaker secretly wants to verify/obtain** and **the casual pretext they speak aloud**.
- **Confrontation / Elicitation**: Never blurt out the accusation immediately. Probe through everyday remarks so the guilty party exposes themselves via defensive over-correction (e.g., deleting a photo, abruptly changing travel dates).
- **Three Tone Drivers**: Always calibrate word choice by **(1) Relationship**, **(2) Setting** (private = direct; public = face-saving/double-entendre), and **(3) Immediate Objective**.
