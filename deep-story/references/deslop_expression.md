# Expression: Making Language Serve the Narrative

This document addresses only how the same fact is phrased. Resolve conflicts first according to [style-resolution.md](deslop_logic_and_focus.md); long sentences, direct emotional statement, parallelism, narrator commentary, and pause punctuation that are explicitly adopted by the author and serve a narrative function may be retained. Word lists serve only as location cues—do not delete word-for-word mechanically, and do not judge authorship based on diction.

## Fix Telegraphic Style and Choppy Short Sentences First

Read ordinary narrative passages continuously first to locate places where action, thought process, and causality are severed. Telegraphic style can also hide inside long sentences: when location, cause, and action each occupy an independent clause strung together merely with commas or conjunctions, it is still item-by-item broadcasting. Having complete information and subject-verb-object structures does not mean the narration is coherent.

Establish each sentence's main clause around the paragraph's central action or change: let each action follow from the previous sentence, and embed background, conditions, and reasons into the action—organizing them via adverbials, attributives, complex clauses, or summary rather than starting a new sentence for every single fact. You do not need to copy the original sentence order verbatim, provided you preserve causality, what the characters know, and information-disclosure boundaries.

> Original: “貨車在南門，她在北門，地址是她發錯的。司機打來電話，問能不能把車開到北門。可北門正在修路，車開不過來，她讓司機在原地等，抱起紙箱往南門跑。”
> (*"The truck was at the south gate, she was at the north gate, and she had sent the wrong address. The driver called and asked if he could drive the truck to the north gate. However, the north gate was under road construction, and the truck couldn't get through, so she told the driver to wait where he was, picked up the cardboard box, and ran toward the south gate."*)

Nothing is missing in terms of information, yet it reads like someone reading off a work order. It can be revised to:

> Revised: “司機照著她發錯的地址開到了南門，打電話過來時，她還在北門。北門這邊正在修路，貨車沒法過來，她只好讓司機留在南門，自己抱起紙箱跑過去。”
> (*"Following the wrong address she had sent, the driver pulled up at the south gate; when he called, she was still at the north gate. With road work blocking the north gate so the truck couldn't get through, she had no choice but to tell the driver to stay at the south gate while she picked up the cardboard box and ran over."*)

Here, the address error becomes the reason the driver went to the wrong gate, the phone call's content is summarized as the character's response, and spatial positions are conveyed alongside the action.

After merging fragmented sentences belonging to the same action, train of thought, or causal chain, each sentence should still center on a single focal point and break only after a complete thought finishes. Restore necessary subjects, objects, and the causal, adversative, conditional, or temporal-sequence relationships already present in the original text—do not try to meet standards by counting merged sentences or padding with function words. Do not add gestures, sensory details, motives, or observation timestamps merely for the sake of coherence.

Read continuously at the end: if every clause is merely re-reporting a state, restructure the sentence or paragraph rather than patching words locally. Likewise, dialogue must not split every condition into a line-by-line Q&A where characters check off information against an outline; see [narrative-focus.md](deslop_logic_and_focus.md) for the division of labor among narration, dialogue, and interiority.

Keep step-by-step presentation for single-sentence paragraphs that carry a complete thought, short dialogue, new clues, shifts in judgment, punchlines/reveals, and deliberate white space; merge only fragmented remnants chopped into three-to-five-character snippets. Restore coherent narration with ordinary transitions, rather than trying to meet a quota by alternating long and short sentences uniformly.

## Diction and Sentence-Pattern Risks Live in Context

Stock phrases often substitute for content that should have been stated clearly, or recur mechanically across different scenes. Circle the original sentence, specify what it obscures or repeats, and then decide whether to delete, compress, or rewrite it. Accurate, ordinary words do not need to be swapped for novel phrasing, and rotating synonyms does not eliminate formulaic templates.

| Risk Pattern | Judgment & Handling |
|---|---|
| Negative setup followed by positive reversal | Patterns like “不是 A，而是 B” (*not A, but B*) or “沒有 X，沒有 Y，只有 Z” (*no X, no Y, only Z*). If the sentence first negates a target no one cares about, state B directly; if it is correcting a mistake, defending/explaining, ruling out a guess, or marking a shift in judgment, retain the necessary contrast. Dropping “而” (*ér*) does not change the judgment. |
| Neat parallelism | Stacking clauses of identical syntax, length, and weight: merge them if they only repeat the same conclusion. Retain if they carry emotional escalation, character voice, or a clear comparison—do not mechanically trim every list down to one item. Expressions like “至於 X 不 X” (*as for whether X or not*), “不 V A，不 V B” (*neither V-ing A nor V-ing B*), and spoken lines should also be judged by function. |
| All-purpose adverbials and stock voice tags | Phrases like “帶著一絲……” (*carrying a trace of...*), “聲音不大，卻不容置疑” (*voice not loud, yet brooking no doubt*), or “平靜得像在念……” (*calm as if reciting...*): if they could be applied to anyone, keep only the recognizable tone or necessary information. An ordinary “說” (*said*) is a neutral tag and does not need to be replaced with a micro-action every time. |
| Scare-quote emphasis | When ordinary nouns are packaged as “機會” (*"opportunity"*), “答案” (*"answer"*), or “蛻變” (*"metamorphosis"*) without any special meaning, remove the quotation marks. Retain them for dialogue, verbatim quotes, codenames, in-world notices/messages, first-time definitions of terms, and clear speaker irony. |
| Template metaphors and abstract grandiosity | Phrases like “潮水般湧來” (*surged in like a tide*) or “命運的齒輪” (*gears of fate*): if they only inflate the tone, compress them back into accurate description. Keep metaphors that convey a character-specific sensation, spatial relationship, or fresh insight; do not swap them for a different batch of metaphors. |
| Bureaucratese and affected profundity | When something straightforward is twisted into “基於當前情況推進後續事項” (*advancing follow-up matters based on the current situation*), revise it to fit the character's identity accurately. Statutory clauses, professional dialogue, historical titles, and system announcements have their own registers—do not flatten all of them into casual chat. |

Words such as “很” (*very*), “才” (*only/just*), “連” (*even*), “還” (*still/also*), “又” (*again*), “只” (*only*), “仿佛” (*as if*), “似乎” (*seemingly*), and “原來” (*as it turned out*) can carry degree, timing, irony, POV uncertainty, or a process of discovery: deleting “才” from “才二十三天” (*only twenty-three days*) alters the weight and emphasis. Modifiers also serve a distinguishing function: “白色藥片” (*white pill*) or “那截鏈子” (*that length of chain*) must not have their color or deictic reference stripped simply because they carry an attributive.

## Voice Comes from How the Character Speaks at This Moment

Read each line of dialogue back in its addressee, setting, and intent: is the character asking for help, brushing someone off, probing, or making a public statement? Honorifics, jargon, circumlocution, repetition, and silence can all be deliberate choices. When everyone speaks in equally complete explanations and equally polished rhetorical questions, restore differentiation using existing character identity, language profiles, and context—not by sprinkling slang, profanity, slips of the tongue, or catchphrases onto everyone.

Polishing must preserve the original communicative intent and information: do not turn an explanation into a provocation, a request into a command, or restraint into losing control. If the basis for why a character speaks this way or why the other party accepts it is missing, route to [story-logic.md](deslop_logic_and_focus.md). For whether a key confrontation has been over-summarized or how many exchanges it should span, route to [narrative-focus.md](deslop_logic_and_focus.md).

## Emotion Can Be Stated Directly; Actions Are Not Responsible for Translating Emotion Words

A line like “他很緊張” (*"He was very nervous"*) may be kept if it is accurate, fits the narrative voice, and sets up the next step. Interiority passages can also present misjudgments, contradictions, memories, and decisions; length, internal monologue, or phrasing like “她感到／意識到” (*"she felt / realized"*) is not grounds for deletion. Delete an emotion-naming sentence only when the surrounding context already conveys the same emotion and naming it adds no new function; psychological shifts that the reader needs to experience should be routed to `narrative-focus` for judgment.

Run a deletion test on every physical reaction one by one: if removed, does the passage lose feeling, characterization, relational meaning, an action condition, or a consequence? Delete stock reactions that merely label an emotion—such as “指尖輕叩” (*fingertips lightly tapping*), “指節泛白” (*knuckles turning white*), or “目光移開” (*gaze shifting away*)—without swapping body parts or replacing them with other micro-actions like rubbing a cup rim (“摩挲杯沿”) or tucking sleeves (“攏袖”); instead, you may revise to state the emotion directly, show the character's mental calculation (“算帳”), or use dialogue. Keep effective details such as an injury preventing someone from holding a pen or a habit being spotted. Do not invent smashing cups, clutching sleeves, locking doors, recording audio, or acting startled just to make the scene feel natural.

## Judge Explanations and the Narrator by Their Contribution

Distinguish—according to the book's POV—between what the character understands at that moment, what information the narrator can provide within that POV, and boundary-crossing foreshadowing/teasers. Limited omniscient POV allows switching viewpoints, which does not mean revealing secrets ahead of time. Even if a sentence pattern permits it, never leak facts that must still remain concealed.

If an explanation supplies the function of a new term, causal linkage, memory trigger, or emotional origin, keep enough of it for comprehension. If it merely re-translates an action just witnessed into a conclusion, or over-explains meaning with “真正重要的是” (*"what truly matters is"*) or “這意味著” (*"this means"*), delete the redundant portion. Do not strip out every “原來” (*as it turned out*) or “因為” (*because*) and force readers to guess missing causes; likewise, do not mechanically convert all normal exposition into dialogue.

When in-scene carriers already exist—such as phones, rule boards, letters, or bills—preserve the information the character actually reads and how it is displayed, handling register issues within the carrier itself first. Do not invent forms or procedures out of thin air merely to carry exposition. If sentences read smoothly yet still fail to explain why a character changes, route to `story-logic`; if there is too much explanation and too little of the key process, route to `narrative-focus`.

## Different Expressions of the Same Fact

Judge the following by context; do not treat them as fill-in-the-blank templates.

| Known Facts & Original Sentence | Acceptable Expression | "De-slopped" on the Surface, Actually Broken |
|---|---|---|
| Keys forgotten at home, will return tomorrow; no one accused her of refusing to return them. Original: “她不是不肯還鑰匙，而是忘在家裡了。‘我明天帶來。’她說。” (*"It's not that she refuses to return the keys, it's that she left them at home. 'I'll bring them tomorrow,' she said."*) | “她把鑰匙忘在家裡了。‘我明天帶來。’她說。” (*"She left the keys at home. 'I'll bring them tomorrow,' she said."*) Removes the useless strawman target; if she is actually responding to an accusation of refusal, retain the corrective function and register the original sentence in the whitelist per [style-resolution.md](deslop_logic_and_focus.md). | “‘愛等就等。’她說。” (*"'Wait if you feel like it,' she said."*) Alters her commitment and attitude. |
| She entered an unlit room, felt her way to the edge of the table, and picked up the pillbox. Original: “她推門。屋里沒開燈。她摸到桌邊。把藥盒拿起來。” (*"She pushed the door. The room's light wasn't on. She felt her way to the table edge. Picked up the pillbox."*) | “進屋後，她摸黑走到桌邊，拿起藥盒。” (*"After entering the room, she felt her way in the dark to the table edge and picked up the pillbox."*) Embeds the lighting into the action; in a tense shot where the original fragmented breaks work, those may also be kept. | “她摸黑吞了兩片藥。” (*"In the dark, she swallowed two pills."*) Changes fetching the medicine into taking it, inventing both quantity and consequence. |

## How Script Alerts Enter Semantic Judgment

The detector is a locating tool. Density alerts are not word-frequency quotas, and `advisory` flags are not completion gates. Having a narrative function does not mean a `blocking` flag can simply be left unedited: original sentences explicitly chosen by the author are preserved via the whitelist; all others should be rewritten naturally while preserving narrative function and re-scanned by the caller—a justification for keeping them cannot substitute for acceptance. When a designated original sentence cannot be changed and is not on the whitelist, report the remaining blocker. Do not evade detection with typos or artificial sentence splitting; deterministic checks for facts, word count, truncation, and placeholders remain subject to caller acceptance and cannot be exempted on grounds of style.

The table below indexes all detector categories; **bold** items are `blocking` by default or at high frequency.

| Alert | Target of Human Judgment |
|---|---|
| **`not-is-comparison`**, **`reverse-not-is`**, **`negation-parade`**, `negation-pair-tic` | See "Negative setup followed by positive reversal" above |
| **`voice-contrast`** | See "All-purpose adverbials and stock voice tags" above |
| **`em-dash`**, `ellipsis-density-tic` | Replace narrator em-dashes with commas, colons, or complete sentences; compress white space when narrator ellipses flood the page |
| **`trailer-ending`**, **`trailer-summary`** | Chapter-end teasers and status summaries—see "Judge Explanations and the Narrator by Their Contribution" above; route ending weight to `narrative-focus` |
| `formulaic-parallelism`, `quote-emphasis-tic` | Whether contrasts and emphasis carry real meaning or merely repeat mechanically |
| `cliche-density-tic`, `metaphor-density-tic` | Whether stock phrases and metaphors collapse into a single monotonous tone or duplicate function |
| `stock-reaction-tic`, `micro-action-tic`, `action-list-tic` | Whether reactions and actions are merely labels or checklists; route prose-allocation issues to `narrative-focus` |
| `period-stutter`, `overcompressed-prose-tic`, `low-connective-density-tic` | Whether connections, objects, and continuous trains of thought are genuinely missing—do not lengthen by count |
| `long-paragraph` | Whether a single paragraph spans multiple shots; break at shot boundaries |
| `abstract-summary-tic`, `reasoning-chain-tic` | Whether summaries are redundant and explanations necessary; route causal gaps to `story-logic` |
| `system-notice-formality-tic` | Whether the register fits the carrier and whether the character can understand its immediate function |

Unflagged prose can still be mechanical; zero hits do not prove that the writing craft or story passes muster.
